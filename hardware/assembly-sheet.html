GPS Tracker Wiring

ESP32-S3 SuperMini build · rev 2 · 2026-10-15 · pins match firmware/include/config.hpp

Bench sheet for assembling the tracker on a breadboard. One table per module, read left to right: module pin, wire, where it goes. Wire colours follow the legend so a photo of the board can be checked against this page.

Before the first wire
  • 3.3 V logic everywhere. The ESP32-S3 pins are not 5 V tolerant. GNSS modules and the OLED output 3.3 V logic even when fed 5 V, so TX/RX/SDA/SCL go straight in.
  • Measure the shield's "3V" rail first. Listings disagree whether it is 3.0 V or 3.3 V. Below 3.1 V, feed the OLED and BMA400 from the board's 3V3 pin instead and give the SD module 5 V (level-shifter type only).
  • Never join the SuperMini's 3V3 pin to the shield's 3V rail. Both are regulator outputs. The board makes its own 3.3 V from its 5V pin.
  • The SuperMini's 5V pin is its USB power line, with no diode. Feed it from the shield only with the USB cable unplugged. On the bench with the console open, power the board from USB and leave that wire off; keep GND common.
  • BIGGA GSM: TX is 5 V logic and the modem draws 2 A bursts. Put 6.8 kΩ from the board's TX pin to GND before it reaches GPIO44. Put C1, a 1000 µF electrolytic, across VSS and GND right at the board's pins, + to VSS: the board's own 100 + 10 + 1 µF are not enough on battery, and without C1 every registration burst resets the ESP32. Use short 22 AWG or thicker wires for VCC/GND.
  • PWRKEY through an RC filter. 1 kΩ in series and 100 nF to GND, both at the ESP end of the wire. A bare wire from the modem's PK pin carried the disturbance of every network attach into the chip and reset it with the task watchdog. Without the parts, tie PK to GND instead and set gsmPowerKeyWired = false.
  • One ground. Every GND goes to the shield's GND. Star it at the shield, not daisy-chained through modules.
  • Check 18650 polarity twice before inserting. A reversed cell burns the shield's protection IC.
  • Antennas. GNSS patch antenna face-up with a sky view; keep the GSM antenna cable away from it.

MicroSD SPI module

J4 · SPI2

Pins run in a straight row on the board: CS 10, SCK 11, MOSI 12, MISO 13. This adapter (6 pins, rated 4.5–5.5 V) is the 5 V kind: it has its own 3.3 V regulator and a level-shifter chip, so VCC goes to a shield 5V pin and the four signals go directly to the board at 3.3 V. On 3.3 V it stays silent and the firmware reports a mount timeout. Card: 8–32 GB, FAT32.

BIGGA GSM (SIM800C) board

J2 · UART2 · C1 1000 µF on VSS · R3 6.8 kΩ on TX · R5/C2 on PK · LED1 + 1 kΩ on NET

Silkscreen names on the board: NET VSS PK RX TX GND on one edge, SPN SPP MICN MICP RING DTR on the other. VSS is the 5 V supply input here, not ground; confirm with the meter that the pin marked GND beeps against the SMA shell and VSS does not. TX idles at 5 V: the board's level shifter is referenced to VCC, so a 6.8 kΩ resistor from TX to GND is required before GPIO44 (gives about 3.0 V). RX needs nothing extra. BOOT is the modem's PWRKEY; the board does not start by itself, the firmware holds BOOT low for a second when the modem is silent, through R5 1 kΩ with C2 100 nF to GND at the ESP end. Feed VCC with 5 V only: 9 V damages the ESP through the shifter, 3.3 V is below the regulator's dropout. Nano-SIM, PIN disabled, 2G coverage confirmed, full-size SMA antenna.

Button module

J7 · 3 pins

A module with VCC, GND and a signal pin. Its own pull-down holds SIG low at rest; a press connects it to VCC, so buttonPressedHigh = true in config.hpp. A bare switch to GND works too, with that setting false. Short press = next display page, hold ≥ 1 s = print diagnostics; also a deep-sleep wake source.

BMA400 (CJMCU-400)

J6 · I2C 0x14 · INT1 → GPIO6 · CS → 3V3

Shares the I2C bus with the OLED. INT1 lands on an RTC-capable pin: it wakes the chip from deep sleep. Tie CS to 3V3: the chip picks I2C or SPI from that pin at power-up, and left to the board's pull-up it came up in SPI mode on one boot in three, silent on the bus. SDO sets the address (low 0x14, high 0x15); the firmware probes both, so tying it to GND is optional but makes the boot log predictable. INT2 stays unconnected.

Assembly order with checks

Add one module at a time and watch the console after each. The expected log line comes from the firmware; if it does not appear, stop and fix that module before adding the next. Ticks are remembered in this browser only.

  1. 5V rail: 4.9–5.2 V · 3V rail: 3.0 or 3.3 V (decides OLED/SD supply)
  2. I (..) tracker: Boot: reset reason power-on (1) I (..) console: Commands on USB Serial/JTAG; type 'help' LED white, then amber once the GNSS is added
  3. a press logs "Button: short press, next page"; diag → battery error counter stays 0; the system page shows the cell voltage
  4. I (..) tracker: State BOOTING -> ACQUIRING I (..) tracker: State ACQUIRING -> TRACKING (sky view, 30–90 s cold) Wrong baud: 'rej' grows in diag → set gnss 1 / save
  5. I (..) ssd1306: Display ready at address 0x3C Tracking page with state, satellites, position
  6. I (..) sdcard: Mounted /sdcard: NNNN MiB 'SD ok' on the screen; TRACKS/YYYYMMDD.CSV appears after the first record
  7. I (..) sim800: Modem silent at every baud rate, pulsing PWRKEY (1/3) (normal: the board needs the pulse) I (..) sim800: Modem answered at 9600 baud (or 115200) I (..) uplink: GPRS up on APN internet 'sent:' grows on the system page; the point appears in Traccar
  8. nmea rx N rej 0 silences 0 | log ok N drop 0 err 0 | up ok N ... | stack free: gps … display … logger … uplink … ui …
